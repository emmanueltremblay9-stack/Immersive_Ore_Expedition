Buried Survey Marker
A marked point of access
"Someone considered this location important enough to measure. Whether they were correct is another matter."
<br><br>
IOE’s survey marker combines chiseled stone bricks, a stone-brick wall and a lantern. A mossy stone border sits partly below the surface; stone paving and a wooden trapdoor cover the shaft opening.
<br><br>
Despite its name, the whole marker is not necessarily hidden underground. Look at the visible stonework and inspect the access rather than assuming every exposed stone marks a deposit.
<br><br>
The marker is a surface component of a connected expedition site. It provides a place to investigate, not a mineral assay or a guarantee of a productive chamber.
<br><br>
The general form described here supplies no hidden coordinates, resource identity or survey result. Establish those facts from the formation you actually examine.
